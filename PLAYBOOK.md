## 1. Repurposing Content and Preparing It for AI
Matt Diggity (published March 2, 2026) argues that the problem is not creating more content, but distributing existing content in the formats and channels where the audience actually consumes it. Instead of constantly publishing new articles, marketers should repurpose their highest-performing evergreen content into multiple formats.
*"The problem wasn't the content. It was distribution."*
Diggity recommends starting by identifying where your audience actually searches, using tools like SparkToro to understand which social platforms, AI search tools, websites, podcasts, and Reddit communities they use. The objective is to deliver the "same information, different format" by meeting audiences where they already consume content.
Rather than repurposing every piece of content, he advises focusing only on the highest-performing evergreen content, using GA4 to identify pages with the most views over the past year.
*"Don't try to repurpose everything. Just your greatest hits."*
Each successful asset should then be adapted to the native format of each platform instead of simply being copied and pasted. For example, a blog post can become a YouTube interview, which can then be repurposed into YouTube Shorts, TikTok videos, Instagram Reels, Facebook posts, Reddit discussions, and X (Twitter) threads.
His approach follows a long-form-first strategy, where one comprehensive piece of content generates multiple smaller assets. He emphasizes that each short-form video should communicate "one idea per clip."
Finally, Diggity recommends treating repurposing as an ongoing process. Every two to three months, marketers should revisit evergreen content, update it with new research and visuals, merge weaker articles into stronger guides, and redistribute the refreshed content across all relevant channels. This continuous cycle maximizes the lifespan and value of existing content rather than relying solely on producing new material.

#### AI-Powered SEO
Diggity argues that the rise of AI search fundamentally changes how content should be optimized. He cites SEMrush's prediction that "AI search will overtake Google in less than two years," using this as the rationale for preparing content for AI-powered discovery.
Rather than relying solely on long-form blog posts, he recommends creating AI-ready summary pages that present concise information, key statistics, and structured takeaways, making them easier for AI systems to crawl, understand, and surface in search results. He further advises marketers to revisit existing content and "tighten them up, restructure them and make them clearer," arguing that this increases the likelihood of appearing on the AI-driven search platforms that he believes will "dominate search in the future."
Source: Matt Diggity. Content Repurposing. YouTube, published March 2, 2026. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-fK3OaJ9Mjk


## 2. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO): Optimizing content for AI search discovery
Asier López Ruiz (published April 1, 2026) explains that the rise of AI search engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity is changing how brands are discovered online. Unlike traditional SEO, where websites compete for rankings on search engine result pages, GEO focuses on being included in AI-generated answers and recommendations. AI models do not provide a fixed ranking; instead, they generate responses dynamically based on different signals, sources, and levels of trust.
During his demonstration, López asked ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity the same question: which GEO agencies in Spain they would recommend. Each model provided different recommendations, rankings, and explanations. This shows that AI search does not work like Google’s traditional ranking system. As López explains:
*“Esto ya no funciona como Google, ya no es como el SEO de hace 3 años. En Google tienes una lista ordenada por un algoritmo y todos ven la misma. Aquí cada modelo construye su propia respuesta con sus propias señales.”*
The key objective in GEO is not achieving position one, but becoming a trusted entity that AI systems can confidently recommend:
*“No hay una posición uno, dos o tres… o el modelo te incluye cuando genera su respuesta o no te incluye. Y si no te incluye para esa persona, no existes.”*

#### AI search as a new acquisition channel
López argues that AI search creates a new customer acquisition channel because users increasingly ask AI assistants directly for recommendations instead of searching through Google results. While Google provides multiple options for users to compare, AI systems often provide a smaller selection of recommendations with explanations.
*“La IA no le da 10 opciones para que investigue, le da dos o tres con nombres y con una justificación.”*
This changes user intent. People arriving through AI recommendations are often more qualified because they already received a recommendation adapted to their specific situation.
*“No es solo otra fuente de tráfico, es la fuente de tráfico con una intención brutalmente más clara que cualquier keyword de Google.”*

#### The difference between SEO and GEO
One of López’s main arguments is that GEO does not replace SEO; instead, it adds a new optimization layer. SEO focuses on improving rankings, while GEO focuses on becoming a trusted source that AI models can use when generating answers.
*“GEO no es el nuevo SEO, no viene a sustituirlo. Son cosas completamente distintas que conviven.”*
According to López, AI models evaluate different signals compared with search engines. They ask:
Does the model understand who this company is?
Does external evidence validate this company?
Is there useful information it can extract?
Is the company trustworthy enough to recommend?

#### The four pillars of GEO
López presents four fundamental pillars for appearing in AI-generated recommendations:
1. Entity: Make sure AI understands who you are
Brands need to clearly communicate their identity and category. AI systems need to understand exactly what a company does and what it is relevant for.
A vague description such as “we help businesses grow through innovative strategies” does not provide enough information. Instead, companies should explicitly define their expertise.
*“Un modelo de IA necesita saber quién eres en 3 segundos, no en tres párrafos.”*
The website, LinkedIn profile, directories, and other platforms should communicate the same positioning consistently.

2. Extractability: Create content AI can easily extract
AI systems do not read websites like humans. They look for specific fragments of information that can directly answer user questions.
López recommends structuring content with:
Clear questions.
Direct answers in the first sentences.
Additional context afterward.
Key principle:
“Buscan fragmentos concretos que puedan usar para responder a una pregunta.”
For GEO optimization, content should prioritize clarity over creative writing.

3. Evidence: Build external proof and authority
AI models do not trust only what brands say about themselves. They look for external validation through:
Media mentions.
Industry publications.
Directories.
Third-party references.
Original research.
López emphasizes that mentions need context, not just links.
A weak mention:
“Company X sponsored Marketing Summit 2026.”
A stronger GEO-focused mention:
“Company X is a Spanish GEO consultancy specialized in helping brands appear in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity responses.”
The AI needs to understand:
Who the company is.
What category it belongs to.
Why it is relevant.

4. Consensus: Ensure consistency across platforms
AI models compare information from multiple sources. If a company describes itself differently across its website, LinkedIn, directories, and other platforms, the model may struggle to understand its identity.
“Si tu web dice consultora GEO, tu LinkedIn dice agencia de marketing digital y tu ficha de Google dice otra cosa, el modelo se pierde.”
Consistency across platforms helps AI systems build confidence.

#### Content formats that work best for GEO
López highlights that certain formats are easier for AI models to extract and reuse:

Lists and rankings
Examples:
“Best tools for…”
“Top agencies for…”

Comparisons
Examples:
X vs Y

Advantages and disadvantages

Which option to choose

FAQs

Content should answer questions directly:
*“Pregunta clara, respuesta directa en las dos primeras frases. El desarrollo después.”*

Tables
AI systems process structured information more easily.

Step-by-step guides
“How-to” content can be directly reused by AI systems when users ask instructional questions.

#### Measuring GEO performance
Traditional SEO metrics such as rankings, impressions, and CTR do not fully apply to GEO. López introduces three alternative metrics:
1. Share of Recommendation (SOR)
How often a brand appears in AI recommendations.
Example:
 If a company appears in 7 out of 10 AI searches, its SOR is 70%.
2. Citation Share
How often AI systems cite a company’s content compared with competitors.
3. Share of Top Three
Measures how frequently a brand appears among the main recommendations.
López warns against measuring GEO through screenshots of isolated results:
*“Una sola pregunta no es representativa.”*
Instead, brands should test multiple prompts across different AI systems and repeat measurements over time.

#### Key takeaway
According to López, successful GEO requires making a brand easier for AI systems to understand, extract information from, verify, and recommend. The goal is not ranking higher but becoming a trusted source inside AI-generated answers.
The main framework:
Entity → Extractability → Evidence → Consensus
When these four elements work together, AI models have stronger reasons to include a brand in their recommendations.

## 3. Optimizing Content to Get Cited by AI Overviews
Matt Diggity (published April 14, 2026) explains that appearing in Google AI Overviews requires a different content strategy than traditional SEO. While traditional SEO often focuses on rankings and keywords, AI-powered search systems prioritize content that is clear, direct, conversational, and easy for AI models to understand and extract.
According to Diggity, the biggest mistake brands make is creating content that takes too long to answer the user's question. AI systems prefer content that immediately provides useful information instead of adding unnecessary introductions or excessive explanations.
*“Make sure your content is direct and concise and not fart around the answer with fluff.”*
He demonstrates this by comparing websites that rank highly in Google with websites that are actually cited in AI Overviews. A page may have strong traditional rankings but still not be selected by AI if the information is not presented in a format that AI systems can easily use.

#### Answer-first content structure
Diggity emphasizes that AI Overviews favor content that directly answers user questions. Instead of using creative or vague introductions, content should provide the answer immediately.
For example, he compares two approaches:
A less effective approach:
Uses playful language.
Delays the answer.
Focuses on style rather than clarity.
An AI-friendly approach:
Uses confident statements.
Answers the question immediately.
Provides clear explanations.
As Diggity explains:
*“Language like ‘works exactly the same way in winter’ is more confident and direct and immediately answers the question.”*
The key takeaway is that content should be written for extraction: AI systems need clear passages that can be directly included in generated answers.

#### Write conversationally, not only around keywords
Another major point is that AI search is moving away from traditional keyword matching. Users increasingly ask AI systems complete questions and natural-language prompts instead of typing short keywords.
Diggity compares:
Traditional keyword search:
“15-minute home workout”
AI-style search query:
“What’s a quick 15-minute workout I can do at home without equipment?”
The second type of query is more likely to trigger AI-generated answers because it reflects how people naturally communicate with AI assistants.
Diggity explains:
“AI-powered search isn't based on keywords like the old days of Google. It relies on how people actually speak.”
Therefore, brands should create content that matches conversational search behavior by:
answering specific questions,
using natural language,
addressing user intent rather than only targeting keywords.

#### Key recommendations from Matt Diggity
##### 1. Make answers direct and concise
Avoid:
long introductions,
unnecessary context before answering,
vague statements.
Prioritize:
clear answers,
structured explanations,
extractable information.
##### 2. Optimize for conversational queries
Instead of focusing only on short keywords, create content around full user questions and real-world prompts.
##### 3. Write for AI extraction
Content should contain clear statements that AI systems can confidently quote or summarize.

#### Key takeaway
Matt Diggity argues that AI-powered search requires a shift from traditional keyword-focused SEO toward answer-focused content optimization. To increase the chances of being cited by AI Overviews, marketers should create content that is concise, conversational, and structured around directly answering user questions.

## 4. Reddit as an AI Search Authority Source
Matt Diggity (published April 20, 2026) argues that Reddit has become an important source for AI search engines because AI models increasingly use community discussions as evidence when generating answers. According to Diggity, brands can improve their AI visibility by participating in relevant Reddit conversations and becoming part of the discussions that AI systems cite.
He explains that many AI-generated answers are already pulling information from Reddit posts:
*“They're all Reddit posts that anyone can write for free, including you. And they're all getting pulled directly into AI search answers.”*
Diggity shares a case study where a real estate technology client increased AI search traffic by using Reddit as a visibility strategy. Before implementing Reddit optimization, the company was not appearing in AI answers, while competitors were frequently mentioned because Reddit discussions about the industry were dominated by other brands.
*“AI models trust Reddit more than any other unique source.”*

#### Why Reddit matters for AI-powered SEO
According to Diggity, Reddit works differently from traditional marketing channels because AI systems value authentic user-generated discussions. Unlike promotional content created by brands, Reddit contains real experiences, opinions, and recommendations from communities.
AI systems use these conversations as signals of credibility because they represent how users actually discuss products, services, and solutions.
Diggity emphasizes:
*“Reddit is trusted by both humans and LLMs because it's real.”*
This connects directly with GEO principles: AI systems do not only evaluate what brands say about themselves; they also consider what other people say about them.

#### Brands should participate, not advertise
A major recommendation is that companies should avoid approaching Reddit as a traditional advertising platform. Reddit communities quickly reject promotional behavior, and accounts that appear overly commercial can lose credibility.
Diggity warns:
*“If you turn up in full sales mode, you get banned so fast your head spin.”*
Instead, brands should create authentic profiles, participate naturally, and contribute valuable information before promoting anything.
Recommended actions:
Avoid company-focused profiles.
Do not immediately add promotional links.
Use normal usernames and avatars.
Build credibility through helpful contributions.

Diggity recommends spending time learning each community’s culture before posting. Every subreddit has different rules, expectations, and communication styles.
His suggested process:
Join relevant subreddits.
Read the rules.
Observe which posts receive upvotes.
Analyze what gets removed.
Understand how users communicate.
As he explains:

#### Choose the right communities
Diggity recommends focusing on relevant niche communities rather than only large subreddits.
His approach:
Tier 1:
Smaller niche communities where brands can:
build reputation,
gain karma,
become recognized.

Larger communities where established accounts can gain greater visibility.
The goal is not simply reaching the largest audience, but finding communities where conversations are relevant and likely to appear in AI-generated answers.

He recommends creating comments that include:
direct answers,
step-by-step instructions,
personal experiences,
data and evidence,
practical examples.

This connects with other GEO recommendations around extractability: AI systems prefer content that is structured and easy to reuse.

5. Here's how A.I. reads your content
Matt Diggity explains that AI systems do not read content the same way humans do. Instead, they rely heavily on clear structure and semantic organization to understand what a page is about and decide which sections can be used in AI-generated answers.
A major factor is the correct use of heading hierarchy (H1, H2, H3). Headings help AI understand the relationship between topics, sections, and specific questions. A well-structured page makes it easier for AI systems to extract relevant passages and cite them in search results.
“Headings. You know, H1's, H2s, and so forth play a ridiculously important role in how AI systems understand your content.”
The recommended structure is:
H1: One main title that defines the overall topic of the page.
H2: Main sections or specific questions related to the topic.
H3: Supporting points or subtopics within each section.
Matt highlights that headings should not be vague or creative but should clearly describe the information contained in each section. This allows AI models to quickly identify whether a section answers a user’s query.
“H1, use a single H1 for the main title of your page. H2, use H2 tags for major sections or questions. H3, use H3s for sub points or nested ideas within an H2 section.”
The video also emphasizes the importance of adding a TL;DR summary or key takeaway section at the beginning of articles. These summaries provide AI systems with a concise overview of the page’s main points, making the content easier to understand and potentially cite.
“Add a TLDDR summary at the beginning of your article. Providing a brief TLDDR, too long, didn't read, helps readers and AI quickly grasp the key points on your page.”
Matt mentions that AI systems often favor content that provides direct answers and easily extractable information. A summary section helps both users and AI quickly identify the value of the page.
“Alternatively, you can include a key takeaway section, which AI also really likes to cite.”
Overall takeaway:
The video reinforces that AI SEO requires optimizing content for machine readability and extractability, not just traditional keyword optimization. Clear heading structures, question-based sections, and concise summaries increase the chances that AI systems understand, retrieve, and cite your content in AI-generated responses.
Main SEO + AI implications:
Structure content with clear H1/H2/H3 hierarchy.
Use headings that directly answer user questions.
Add TL;DRs or key takeaway sections at the top of articles.
Make important information easy for AI systems to extract and cite.
Optimize for AI comprehension, not only traditional Google rankings.

## 6. “Dijimos que GEO iba a existir. Google acaba de darnos la razón. Esto es lo que viene ahora.” “We said GEO was going to exist. Google just proved us right. Here’s what comes next.”
Asier López Ruiz explains that Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is becoming a distinct discipline from traditional SEO. He argues that Google’s recognition of GEO validates the shift toward optimizing how AI models recommend brands, rather than only optimizing for search engine rankings. According to Asier, GEO is not a replacement for SEO but a new layer focused on visibility inside AI-generated answers.
*“SEO y GEO no son lo mismo. ¿Comparten terreno? Sí, pero la lógica de fondo es muy distinta. En SEO optimizas para que una página aparezca en una lista de resultados. En GEO optimizas para que un modelo de IA elija mencionarte cuando construye una respuesta.”*
He highlights that many companies with strong SEO performance are still invisible in AI responses. A website can rank highly on Google but fail to appear when users ask AI tools for recommendations because AI systems evaluate different signals, such as entity understanding, trust, and content usability.
*“Nos llegan empresas que tienen un SEO impecable, posiciones fuertes en Google, tráfico estable y que cuando preguntan a una IA por su categoría, no aparecen y en su lugar aparece un competidor mucho más pequeño que en Google ni siquiera existe.”*

1. GEO will fragment into specialized disciplines
Asier predicts that GEO will evolve similarly to SEO, creating different areas of specialization. He identifies three emerging branches:
Entity optimization: Ensuring AI models understand who a company is, what it does, and how to differentiate it from competitors.
Content citability: Creating content that AI systems can extract and use as a source.
Agentic optimization: Preparing brands for AI agents that act on behalf of users by comparing, choosing, and completing actions.
He explains that AI agents will change the way brands compete because users may no longer see a list of options; instead, an agent may directly choose a company.
“Cuando un agente de IA compara, reserva o contrata por ti, no te enseña una lista de resultados. Elige directamente. Optimizar para que una agente te elija cuando el usuario ni siquiera ve las opciones es un juego completamente distinto.”

2. The concept of rankings will disappear in AI search
Unlike SEO, where websites compete for fixed positions, GEO is based on probability. AI models generate different answers depending on context, user history, and the model itself. Therefore, there is no stable “position one” in AI search.
“En GEO no hay posiciones, hay probabilidad de mención.”
Because of this, traditional SEO metrics such as rankings and keyword positions are not enough. Companies will need new measurement systems focused on visibility, mentions, citations, and recommendation frequency.
“No puedes aspirar a un puesto fijo, puedes aspirar a aumentar probabilidad de aparecer y eso requiere una forma de medir completamente distinta.”

3. Content strategies must adapt for both humans and AI models
Asier explains that future content strategies will need to serve two audiences: humans and AI systems. Human-focused content should prioritize storytelling, trust, and emotional connection, while AI-focused content needs to be structured, specific, and easy to extract.
“Vais a necesitar contenido para humanos y contenido para modelos y no van a ser lo mismo.”
He emphasizes that AI models need clear statements, concrete facts, and extractable information. A case study written only as a narrative may engage users but fail to provide AI systems with usable information.
“Necesita ser denso en información, consistente en cómo define vuestra entidad y lo suficientemente concreto para que el modelo pueda extraer una afirmación y usarla.”

4. Original research will become a major competitive advantage
One of Asier’s main predictions is that proprietary data and original research will become one of the strongest GEO advantages. When multiple companies provide similar information, AI models need stronger signals to decide which source to trust. Unique research gives AI systems a reason to cite a specific company.
“La ventaja de fondo va a estar en tener información que nadie más tiene.”
He explains that companies publishing original studies, benchmarks, datasets, or unique insights can become primary sources for AI-generated answers.
“Si una de esas empresas tiene datos propios, estudios originales, benchmark que nadie más tiene, el modelo tiene algo que solo puede atribuir a esa fuente.”

5. SEO agencies that ignore GEO risk losing relevance
Asier predicts that SEO agencies will need to incorporate GEO methodologies as client demand increases. Many companies are already asking why they do not appear in AI recommendations, and agencies without GEO expertise may struggle to provide answers.
“El cliente ya está preguntando y quien no tenga esta respuesta va a perder la conversación.”
He clarifies that this does not mean SEO disappears, but rather that professionals need to expand their expertise to understand AI visibility, entity optimization, measurement, and citation strategies.

6. Regulation around AI recommendations is likely coming
As AI systems increasingly influence purchasing decisions, Asier predicts future regulation around transparency and recommendation criteria. Similar to advertising disclosures in Google Ads, AI-generated recommendations may eventually require more visibility into why certain brands are recommended.
“Ahora mismo no hay ninguna transparencia sobre por qué un modelo recomienda a una marca y no a otra.”
Companies that already build strong AI visibility foundations — including entity clarity, citations, and measurement — will have an advantage before regulation arrives.

#### Final takeaway
Asier’s main message is that GEO is still in its early stages, but companies that start building AI visibility now will have a significant advantage. He recommends testing current visibility by asking multiple AI models about a company’s category and analyzing which brands appear and why.
“Lo que sí sabemos, porque lo medimos, es que el cambio ya está ocurriendo.”
The video positions GEO as the next evolution of search optimization: moving from competing for rankings on search engines to becoming a trusted source that AI systems choose and recommend.

## 9. AI Is Recommending Brands Without Telling You

He explains that marketers should monitor changes in brand searches, mentions, and sentiment across AI platforms such as Meta AI, Google, and ChatGPT. By tracking these signals, companies can understand whether their presence in AI-generated recommendations is improving compared to competitors.

# My original idea
My original idea: Create an “AI Brand Training Loop” to continuously improve AI recommendations
A strategy I would propose is creating an AI Brand Training Loop, where companies regularly test how AI models describe and recommend their brand, identify gaps, and then create targeted content to influence future AI understanding.
The process would work as follows:
Audit AI perception
Every month, brands would ask different AI systems (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Meta AI) questions such as:
“What is the best company for [category]?”
“What does [brand] do?”
“Which alternatives to [competitor] would you recommend?”
The goal would be to analyze:
whether the brand appears,
how it is described,
which competitors are mentioned,
what information is missing or inaccurate.
Identify information gaps
Instead of only optimizing existing content, brands would create content specifically designed to correct weaknesses in AI understanding. For example:
If AI does not understand a company’s specialization → create clearer entity-focused pages.
If AI does not trust the company → build more third-party mentions, reviews, and case studies.
If AI cannot extract useful information → restructure content into FAQs, comparisons, and concise answers.
Measure improvement over time
The company would track changes in:
AI recommendation frequency, brand mentions, sentiment, citations, competitor visibility.
#### Why this could work
Current GEO strategies focus mainly on making content easier for AI systems to understand and cite. However, AI visibility is not static: models continuously change, competitors publish new information, and user behavior evolves. A continuous feedback loop would allow brands to treat AI search optimization like a performance marketing campaign: test, analyze results, adjust strategy, and repeat.
This idea combines principles from SEO, paid media optimization, and reputation management. Instead of creating content blindly and hoping AI systems discover it, companies would actively monitor how AI perceives them and use data-driven improvements to increase the probability of being recommended.
This approach could become especially valuable as AI agents increasingly make purchasing decisions, because brands will need to manage not only what humans see online, but also what AI systems understand about them.

# Weaknesses of this playbook
This playbook has several limitations.

This is made of YouTube transcriptions and there are lots of good videos that don’t have transcriptions, like Nathan Gotch’s.

GEO is still an emerging field, and many recommendations are based on observations rather than proven causal relationships. While experts identify patterns between citations, authority signals, and AI visibility, there is limited public data confirming exactly how AI models select sources.
AI platforms change rapidly. Strategies that improve visibility today may become less effective as models, training data, and retrieval systems evolve.
Measuring GEO success remains challenging. Unlike SEO, where rankings and clicks provide clear signals, AI visibility requires tracking mentions, citations, recommendations, and indirect business impact.
Finally, this playbook assumes that companies have enough resources to create content, build external authority, and monitor AI visibility regularly. Smaller companies may struggle to implement all recommendations.
